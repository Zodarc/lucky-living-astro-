# eufy FamiLock E34 Review: Is Palm-Vein Unlocking Worth It?

Smart locks have become increasingly flexible.

You can unlock some with fingerprints. Others use a phone, PIN, NFC card, Apple Home Key, or even traditional keys.

The **eufy FamiLock E34** takes a different route.

Its headline feature is **palm-vein recognition**, allowing users to unlock the door without touching the lock or taking out a phone.

It sounds futuristic.

But is it actually useful?

And more importantly, is it worth the E34's $279.99 list price?

## Quick Verdict

The eufy FamiLock E34 is one of the more interesting premium smart locks available because palm-vein recognition gives it a genuinely different user experience.

It also has:

- Built-in Wi-Fi
- Matter over Thread
- A 10,000mAh rechargeable battery
- A 750mAh backup battery
- PIN access
- Physical-key backup

But there are meaningful compromises.

The E34 does not support Apple Home Key, lacks door-position sensing, and costs considerably more than the Aqara U200 at current US list prices.

**Our verdict: excellent for the right buyer, but not the automatic best smart lock.**

## What Is the eufy FamiLock E34?

The E34 is a connected smart deadbolt from eufy.

Its main differentiator is palm-vein recognition.

Rather than requiring users to touch a fingerprint sensor, the lock scans the user's palm and uses the registered biometric profile to authenticate access.

eufy says recognition can happen in approximately 0.6 seconds and supports up to 50 palm-vein users.

Those figures come from eufy and should be treated as manufacturer claims rather than independent Spoky Curates test results.

## Why Palm-Vein Recognition Is Interesting

Most biometric smart locks use fingerprints.

Palm-vein recognition approaches the problem differently.

The user does not have to press a finger against a sensor.

That can be useful when:

- Your hands are full
- Your fingers are wet
- You do not want to touch a shared surface
- Multiple family members need convenient access

The technology is therefore more than a marketing gimmick.

The question is whether it works consistently enough in everyday use to justify paying more.

Independent owner reports are mixed, which is why we would not treat eufy's recognition claims as guaranteed real-world performance.

## Battery Life

Battery anxiety is one of the biggest concerns with smart locks.

The E34 attempts to address that with a large battery system.

It uses:

**10,000mAh main battery**

plus:

**750mAh integrated backup battery**

eufy claims up to eight months of battery life from the main battery.

There is also USB-C emergency power.

That gives the E34 a reassuring amount of redundancy.

However, the eight-month figure is a manufacturer estimate. Actual performance will depend on how frequently the lock is used, wireless conditions, temperature, and other variables.

## Does the E34 Need a Hub?

For eufy's own remote-control experience, the E34 has built-in Wi-Fi.

That is convenient because you do not need to purchase a separate Wi-Fi bridge solely to get remote access.

Matter is different.

If you want to use the lock with third-party Matter ecosystems, eufy says a compatible Matter controller is required.

## Does It Work With Apple Home?

Yes, but there is an important distinction.

The E34 can integrate with Apple Home through Matter.

However:

**The E34 does not support Apple Home Key.**

That means you cannot use the same tap-to-unlock iPhone or Apple Watch experience available on supported Home Key locks.

For Apple-centric households, this makes the Aqara U200 considerably more interesting.

## E34 vs Aqara U200

The Aqara U200 is probably the most important comparison for the E34.

The two locks approach smart entry differently.

The E34 focuses on palm-vein recognition.

The U200 offers:

- Apple Home Key
- Fingerprint
- PIN
- NFC
- App access
- Physical key access
- Matter over Thread

Aqara also uses a retrofit-oriented design.

At the time of this research, Aqara lists the U200 at $169.99 in the United States, while eufy lists the E34 at $279.99.

That is a significant difference.

If palm-vein recognition is not important to you, the U200 may offer the better overall value.

## Installation and Compatibility

The E34 is designed for compatible standard doors and eufy lists approximately 35–55mm door thickness compatibility.

As with any smart lock, buyers should verify their existing door and deadbolt configuration before purchasing.

Do not assume that a smart lock will fit simply because your door has a standard-looking deadbolt.

## Security and Privacy

eufy lists ANSI/BHMA Grade 1 certification and IP65 protection.

The company also states that palm-vein information is stored locally.

Those are positive specifications.

But smart-lock security is broader than the lock cylinder.

Users should also consider:

- Account security
- Software updates
- Wireless security
- Access permissions
- Installation quality
- Physical door security

## The Auto-Lock Problem

The E34 can automatically lock after a configured period.

But it does not include a dedicated door-position sensor.

That means the lock cannot independently confirm that the door is fully closed before relying on its timed locking behavior.

For some households this will not matter.

For others, particularly homes where doors are frequently left partially open, it is an important limitation.

## Pros and Cons

### Pros

- Innovative palm-vein unlocking
- Large 10,000mAh battery
- Integrated backup battery
- Built-in Wi-Fi
- Matter over Thread
- Multiple access methods
- No required monthly subscription

### Cons

- Expensive
- No Apple Home Key
- Requires Matter controller for third-party Matter integration
- No dedicated door-position sensor
- Manufacturer battery/recognition claims are not independently verified
- Mixed independent feedback

## Who Is the E34 For?

The E34 is best suited to buyers who want something beyond a conventional fingerprint smart lock.

It makes particularly good sense for:

**Families**

Multiple household members can have their own palm-vein profile.

**Smart-home enthusiasts**

Matter support makes the E34 relevant to multi-platform homes.

**eufy users**

Existing eufy customers may appreciate keeping the lock within the same ecosystem.

**People who value contactless access**

If avoiding physical contact with a fingerprint reader is important, palm-vein recognition is an interesting solution.

## Who Should Buy the Aqara U200 Instead?

The Aqara U200 is probably the better choice if you:

- Use Apple Home
- Want Apple Home Key
- Want fingerprint unlocking
- Want NFC
- Want a retrofit installation
- Want to spend less

## Final Verdict

The eufy FamiLock E34 is a **strong but specialized smart lock**.

Its palm-vein recognition gives it a genuinely distinctive advantage, while its large battery, backup battery, built-in Wi-Fi, and Matter support make the overall hardware package compelling.

But the premium price means buyers should know exactly why they are choosing it.

If you want palm-vein unlocking, the E34 is worth serious consideration.

If you simply want the most flexible smart lock at a lower price, the Aqara U200 is likely the better choice.

**Spoky Curates Rating: 4.1/5**

The E34 is not the smart lock everyone needs.

It is the smart lock worth considering when **contactless palm-vein access is exactly what you want.**
