Three hubs cover most households right now: the **Aqara Hub M3** for anyone mixing ecosystems, the **Amazon Echo Hub** for Alexa-and-Ring households that want a screen, and the **SmartThings Station** for people who just need Matter and Thread support without paying for a display. None of them is a bad hub — the right one just depends on what you're already running.

## Quick Comparison

| Price | $119.99–159.99 | $179.99 (often $110–120 on sale) | $59.99 |
| Screen | No | 8-inch touchscreen | No |

A hub's job also changed shape recently: it's not just bridging Zigbee or Z-Wave devices anymore, it's acting as a Matter controller and — increasingly — a Thread border router, since more sensors and locks now ship as Thread-only and won't function without one. All three hubs here cover that baseline. What separates them is ecosystem lock-in and whether you want a screen.

## Aqara Hub M3 — the one to buy if you're not locked into an ecosystem

This is the pick that wins by not picking a side. Plug it in, scan a QR code, and it announces itself to Apple Home, Google Home, and Alexa simultaneously over Matter — you're not choosing one ecosystem the way you are with the other two hubs here. The Zigbee 3.0 radio handles Aqara's own sensors and switches directly, the Thread border router covers newer Thread-only gear, and the IR blaster means your old TV and window AC unit can join the automations too. Wired Ethernet is a nice touch for a device this central to the house.

It's not flawless: gear from Philips Hue or TP-Link Kasa has to come in through its own Matter bridge rather than the M3's Zigbee radio directly, and there's no Z-Wave support, so an existing Z-Wave thermostat won't connect through it.

## Amazon Echo Hub — the one with a screen

The Echo Hub is a different kind of product entirely: an 8-inch wall-mounted touchscreen rather than a hidden puck. It covers the same Matter, Thread, and Zigbee bases as the other two, but it's built for the Alexa ecosystem specifically and won't talk to Google Home or Apple HomeKit natively. What justifies the price tag is the interface, not the protocol list — a persistent dashboard for lights, locks, and thermostats, and its clear strongest use case: live Ring camera feeds without pulling out a phone. If you don't have three or more Ring devices, that advantage mostly disappears and you're paying a screen tax for hub functionality the SmartThings Station covers for a third of the price.

Worth noting: it regularly drops to $110–120 during sales events, which changes the math considerably.

## SmartThings Station — the budget pick

At $59.99, this is a third of the Echo Hub's price for the same core protocol coverage: Matter, Thread, and Zigbee. No screen, no dashboard — just the hub functionality plus a wireless charging pad built into the top. It's the obvious choice for Samsung households, and it's also a sensible add-on if you've already got a working Zigbee setup and just need Matter support bolted on without buying a whole new ecosystem.

## Which One Should You Buy?

**If you're not committed to a single ecosystem yet, get the Aqara Hub M3.** It's the only one of the three that talks to Apple, Google, and Amazon's platforms on day one, and the price difference versus the Echo Hub is easily justified by that flexibility alone.

**If you're already deep in Alexa and Ring, get the Echo Hub** — but only if you actually have multiple Ring devices to look at. Otherwise you're paying for a screen you won't use much.

**If you just need Matter and Thread added to an existing setup and don't care about a display, get the SmartThings Station.** It's the cheapest way into this generation of hubs, full stop.

Whichever you pick, plan to live with it for a few years. Re-pairing a dozen devices and rebuilding automations because you switched ecosystems is a real cost, not a hypothetical one — so match the hub to the ecosystem you actually intend to stay in.
