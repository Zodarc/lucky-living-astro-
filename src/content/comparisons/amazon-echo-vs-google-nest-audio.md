Both the Amazon Echo (4th Gen) and the Google Nest Audio are excellent smart speakers that do the fundamentals well: they fill a room with sound, respond accurately to voice commands, and integrate tightly with their respective smart home ecosystems. Where they differ is in price, ecosystem depth, and a few hardware features that matter more than they might first appear.

## Sound Quality

The Nest Audio has a noticeable audio edge. Its larger 75mm woofer and 19mm tweeter produce a fuller, more room-filling sound with better stereo separation. The Echo holds its own for casual listening and podcasts but sounds slightly compressed at higher volumes. If music is your primary use case, the Nest Audio wins.

## Smart Home Integration

The Echo wins here, and it is not close. The built-in Zigbee hub means you can pair Philips Hue bulbs, smart plugs, and many other devices directly without a separate hub — saving $50–$80 up front. Alexa's third-party device compatibility is also broader than Google Assistant's at the time of writing.

## Voice Assistant

This comes down to your existing ecosystem. If you live in Google (Calendar, Maps, Gmail), Google Assistant is more useful day-to-day. If you shop on Amazon and use Prime, Alexa's shopping and household routines integrations are more polished. Neither is clearly better in isolation.

## Value

The Echo (4th Gen) is available as a single unit at a fraction of the Nest Audio 3-pack price. Unless you specifically need three synchronized speakers, the Echo offers more practical value.

## Verdict

Pick the **Amazon Echo (4th Gen)** if you want the best all-round value, a Zigbee hub, and an Alexa-first home. Pick the **Google Nest Audio** if audio quality or Google ecosystem depth matters more than price.
